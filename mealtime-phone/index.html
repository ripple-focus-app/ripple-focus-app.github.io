Dinner turns into everyone watching their own phone

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. This page is for adults who end up scrolling through every meal, and for parents whose child will only eat with a video playing.

Why meals are easy to lose

A meal is short and you sit still with one hand free, so reaching for a short video feels harmless. Twenty minutes later the food is cold and nobody has talked. Putting the phone in another room works when you remember. A fixed schedule helps on the days you forget.

Android, for yourself

Ripple for Android (v0.1.22, Android 8.0 or later) can start a focus session right away from the Today page. Choose to lock only the apps you pick, such as short video and game apps, and pick 25, 45, 60, 90 or 120 minutes or a custom length. Calls, messages and the camera keep working. If dinner is at the same time every day, add a schedule such as 18:30 to 19:30. During a schedule only the apps on your allowed list open, so keep the phone dialer and your chat app on that list. Loosening a rule early needs a 15 minute wait by default, which you can change to 30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours, or an unlock code.

Android, for a child

On the child's phone or tablet, add a mealtime schedule and leave the video and game apps off the allowed list. Keep the unlock code yourself. Turning on "unlock code only" also turns on uninstall protection, and changing the clock does not end the schedule early. Ripple has no remote management, so you set it up in person on the child's device. Ripple needs the accessibility permission, and on some phones you also have to allow it to run in the background.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5 (iOS 16 or later). It blocks chosen apps on a schedule, prevents deleting apps, resists clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no one tap sessions and no daily limits, so set the usual mealtimes as a schedule.
