Making the phone stop you after a long session

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker with no ads and no account, so keep that in mind. A daily limit of two hours often gets spent in one sitting after dinner. What helps many people more is a pause in the middle: after a set time in a row, the app closes for a few minutes, and then you can go back if you still want to.

What Take a Break does on Android

Ripple for Android v0.1.29 has a setting called Take a Break. You pick how long you can use apps in a row and how long the rest is, for example 30 minutes of use and 5 minutes of rest. When the time is up, the limited apps cannot be opened during the rest. After the rest you can carry on. You can also give one app its own Take a Break setting from its daily limit page.

In v0.1.29 I fixed a bug where the keyboard counted as switching apps, so the timer started over while you typed. Typing time now counts, which matters for long chats and comment threads.

For yourself

Start with something easy to keep, such as 30 minutes on and 5 off, and put your short video and game apps under it. If you want to loosen the rule later, Ripple makes you wait first, 15 minutes by default, with 30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours as options, or you can enter an unlock code that someone else keeps.

For a child

For a younger child, 20 minutes on and 10 off is a common starting point. Add a daily limit for games and videos and a time window for homework and sleep. Keep the unlock code yourself and turn on strict mode. On Android 11 and later, Anti-Uninstall stops the app from being removed, and Ripple also resists changing the clock. There is no remote control from your own phone. Everything is set on the child's phone.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is still v0.1.5 and needs iOS 16 or later. It has no Take a Break and no daily limit. It can block apps during time windows you choose, prevent deleting apps, resist clock changes and use an unlock code. A short window such as 21:00 to 21:15 can work as a fixed pause in the evening.

What it cannot do

Ripple only limits app time. It is not a medical tool, and it cannot say anything about eyesight. The keyboard fix was checked on an emulator, not yet on every phone brand.
