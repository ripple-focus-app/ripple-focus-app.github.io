Scrolling on the toilet

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. This page is about a small habit that adds up: taking the phone into the bathroom, opening a video app, and coming out half an hour later with sore legs. Some parents notice the same thing with a child who goes to the bathroom during homework and stays there.

Without any app

Leave the phone outside the door. That one rule fixes most of it. If that feels too hard, keep a book or a magazine in the bathroom so your hands have something to hold. Short video apps are the usual problem because each clip ends just as the next one starts, so there is never a natural point to stop.

Android, for yourself

Ripple for Android (v0.1.26, Android 8.0 or later) can limit how many times a day an app opens. Most bathroom scrolling starts with one more open of the same app, so a cap of a few opens a day cuts it down. You can also make each open start with a pause of 5 to 30 seconds, which is often enough to put the phone down. A daily limit stops the app once the day's time is used, and daily limits can differ by weekday.

Loosening a rule needs a wait of 15 minutes by default (30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours can be chosen) or an unlock code. Making a rule stricter works right away. If you give the code to someone else and turn on strict mode, and Anti-Uninstall on Android 11 and later, the limit is hard to undo in the moment.

Android, for a child

If a child takes the phone into the bathroom during homework, set a homework schedule, for example 7 pm to 9 pm, that blocks games and video apps in that window. Add a daily limit for the rest of the day and keep the unlock code yourself. Ripple has no remote management, so you set it on the child's phone in person, and it is better to tell the child what you set.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5 (iOS 16 or later). It blocks chosen apps during time windows, prevents deleting apps, resists clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no open limits, no pause before opening and no daily limits. For an adult, a schedule over the times you usually drift, such as after lunch or late evening, is the closest match. For a child, a homework schedule works the same way as on Android.
