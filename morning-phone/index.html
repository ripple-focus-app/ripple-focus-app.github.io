The alarm goes off and the scrolling starts

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. This page is for adults who lose the first half hour of the day in bed with their phone, and for parents whose child watches videos when they should be getting dressed for school.

Why the morning goes first

The phone is already in your hand because it was the alarm. You open one app to check the time or the weather, and then a feed is right there. Leaving the phone across the room helps. A morning schedule helps on the days the phone ends up on the pillow anyway.

Android, for yourself

Ripple for Android (v0.1.22, Android 8.0 or later) lets you add a schedule such as 6:30 to 8:00 on weekdays. During a schedule only the apps on your allowed list open, so put the clock app, the dialer, maps and your chat app on that list and leave short video and game apps off. On days when you wake up at a different time, start a focus session from the Today page instead, lock only the apps you pick, and choose 25, 45, 60, 90 or 120 minutes or a custom length. Loosening a rule early needs a 15 minute wait by default, which you can change to 30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours, or an unlock code. Keep the clock app on the allowed list so you can still turn off the alarm.

Android, for a child

On the child's phone or tablet, add a school morning schedule and leave videos and games off the allowed list. Keep the unlock code yourself. Turning on "unlock code only" also turns on uninstall protection, and moving the clock does not end the schedule early. Ripple has no remote management, so you set it up in person on the child's device. Ripple needs the accessibility permission, and on some phones you also need to let it run in the background.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5 (iOS 16 or later). It blocks chosen apps on a schedule, prevents deleting apps, resists clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no one tap sessions and no daily limits, so set your usual morning hours as a schedule.
