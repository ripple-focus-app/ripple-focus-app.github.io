A first phone for your child

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. This page is for parents about to give a child a first phone, often at the start of middle school, who want some rules in place on day one. It also has a short part for parents who want to set the same kind of limits on their own phone.

Before you hand it over

Agree on a few things first and write them down: where the phone charges at night, when it stays in the school bag, and how long games and videos get on school days and at weekends. Rules agreed before the phone arrives are easier to keep than rules added after the first fight. Set up the phone together with your child, so nothing feels hidden.

Android, on the child's phone

Ripple for Android (v0.1.26, Android 8.0 or later) works on the phone itself. You can give games and short video apps 0 minutes on school days and a set time at weekends, since daily limits can differ by weekday. A school hours schedule and a bedtime schedule block chosen apps in those windows. Blocked websites covers sites opened in the browser (tested mainly in Chrome).

Loosening a rule needs a wait of 15 minutes by default (30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours can be chosen) or the unlock code, which you keep. Strict mode and Anti-Uninstall on Android 11 and later make it harder for a child to remove the limits. Ripple has no remote management and no location or message monitoring, so rules are set in person on the child's phone. If you need to change something, sit down with the phone.

Android, for yourself

Children copy what they see. If you also want to cut back, set a daily limit or an open limit on your own video apps, with each open starting with a short pause of 5 to 30 seconds. It is easier to ask a child to keep a rule when you have one too.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5 (iOS 16 or later). On a child's iPhone it blocks chosen apps during time windows, such as school hours and bedtime, prevents deleting apps, resists clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no daily limits, no open limits and no website blocking. Apple's own Screen Time and Family Sharing cover other parts, such as purchase approval.
