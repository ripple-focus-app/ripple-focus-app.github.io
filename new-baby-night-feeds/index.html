A new baby, night feeds, and two phones to manage

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker for Android and iPhone with no ads and no account, so keep that in mind. In the first months with a baby, many parents tell the same story. A feed at 3:00 takes forty minutes, the phone is in one hand, and short videos fill the time. The baby falls asleep and the parent keeps scrolling for another half hour, then the next feed comes. During the day an older brother or sister gets the tablet or phone for much longer than before, because the adults have no energy left to argue.

Your phone during night feeds

Blocking the phone completely at night does not fit here. You may want a white noise app, a feeding tracker, a message to your partner, or a call. With Ripple on Android you pick which apps are limited, so the feed tracker and messages stay open while short video and shopping apps are limited.

A daily limit is usually kinder than a night window. Give the short video app, for example, 20 minutes between 0:00 and the morning feed, and let the rest of the day have its own limit. Since v0.1.25 a limit can differ for each day of the week, so the nights your partner takes the feeds can be stricter. An open count limit also helps: if the app can only be opened 3 times a day, picking it up on reflex at every feed runs out fast.

When a limit is reached, the block page offers sleep sounds and slow breathing. Some parents use these to settle back down after the baby sleeps. Long playback with the screen off, during a call or after unplugging headphones has not been tested on a real phone, so do not rely on it as the baby's white noise.

Loosening a rule at 3:00 when you are exhausted is easy, so Ripple makes you wait first: 15 minutes by default, with 30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours as other choices. Tightening a rule takes effect right away.

The older child while you are busy with the baby

Ripple has no remote control and no parent app, so you set the rules once on the child's phone or tablet. A daily limit for games and short videos, with a bit more on weekends, means you do not have to negotiate every time you sit down to feed. A window such as 20:30 to 7:00 on school nights keeps the evening for sleep. Calls, texts and alarms are not blocked.

Keep the unlock code to yourself. With "unlock code only" turned on (the code must be 8 to 12 digits), waiting cannot loosen the rules, and there are at most 3 successful unlocks a day. On Android 11 and later, turn on uninstall protection. Changing the date or time does not add time.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5 and needs iOS 16 or later. It can block apps during time windows you choose, stop deleting apps through Screen Time, resist clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, no open count limits and no sleep sounds. For a parent on iPhone, a window for the hours after midnight covers the night feeds, and the system Screen Time app limits can handle daily totals.

What it cannot do

Ripple only works on the phone it is installed on and does not filter websites. Rules and usage records stay on the phone. It will not make the nights shorter. It only makes the phone less of a trap during them.
