Remote parental control or free app limits on the phone itself?

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so read this with that in mind. Facts about OurPact come from ourpact.com, its help pages and the US App Store, and facts about Norton Family from norton.com and its support pages, in November 2026. Prices vary by country and change.

The first question is whether you need to change the rules when the phone is not in your hand. If you do, you want a parental control service with a parent app. If you can set things up in person, or the phone is your own, a free app on the phone may be enough.

OurPact

OurPact has parent apps for iPhone and Android and a website, and it manages a child's iPhone, iPad and Android devices. Premium is $6.99 a month or $69.99 a year in the US for up to 20 child devices. It has block schedules, instant blocks, a daily screen time allowance, app rules (always blocked, always allowed or blocked by schedule), web restrictions, text blocking, alerts for new apps and for removal, and location with 7 days of history. Premium+ at $9.99 a month or $99.99 a year adds screenshots of the child's screen. There is a 14 day trial and a small free tier for one device. Its help page says it does not support Xiaomi, Motorola, TCL, Honor, OnePlus, Oppo, Tecno or ZTE phones.

Norton Family

Norton Family is a subscription, $49.99 for the first year in the US, with a 30 day trial. It works on a child's Windows PC, Android, iPhone and iPad. It shows sites and searches, blocks unsuitable sites, sets allowed and blocked hours and sends reports. Blocking chosen apps and a daily device time limit work on Android and Windows. On iPhone its support pages say there is no daily limit and no app supervision.

Ripple

Ripple is free with no ads and no account, and nothing leaves the phone. On Android 8.0 or later (v0.1.20) every app gets its own daily amount, and 0 minutes keeps an app closed all day. It also has schedules, open limits, a break rule, quick focus, blocked websites and a usage calendar. Loosening a rule or removing the app needs a 15 minute wait or the unlock code. In strict mode only the code works, at most 3 successful uses a day, and on Android 11 or later the app cannot be uninstalled while it is on. A forgotten code is lifted 72 hours after a request. Ripple for iPhone (v0.1.5) only blocks apps by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. Ripple has no parent app, no location, no screenshots and no reports, so you change rules on the phone itself.

For a child's phone

If your teen is out most of the day and you want to change rules or see where they are, OurPact or Norton Family fit better. If you mostly want one game limited to 30 minutes and a short video app kept closed on school days, and you can hold the phone to set it up, Ripple does that for free. Keep the unlock code yourself. Ripple also runs on Xiaomi, Oppo, Honor and OnePlus phones, which OurPact skips, though some of them close background apps, so follow the keep alive guide. For a child on iPhone, the built in Screen Time with Family Sharing is free and does more than Ripple for iPhone.

For your own phone

OurPact and Norton Family are designed around a parent managing a child. If you want limits on your own phone, Ripple is set up where you use it. Give yourself 20 minutes of Instagram a day, and hand the strict mode code to a partner or friend if you know you will try to undo it.
