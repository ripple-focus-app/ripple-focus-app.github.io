Android 17 parental controls without Family Link, and what to use on older phones

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. The facts about Android 17 below come from Google's help page Set up parental controls on an Android device, read in November 2026.

What Android 17 added

On Android 17 or later there is a Parental controls entry in Settings. You set a PIN of at least 4 digits and can add a recovery email. After that you can set a daily limit for the whole phone, downtime hours, a time limit per app, blocked apps, web filters for Chrome and the Google app, and age filters for app stores. When the limit or downtime starts the phone locks, and entering the PIN adds bonus time. Changing the date and time, adding users and factory reset also ask for the PIN. No Google account has to be signed in for this, so it works for a child who has no account of their own.

What to know before you rely on it

It needs Android 17. Many phones in use today, including a lot of Xiaomi, Oppo, vivo and Samsung models, are still on Android 14 to 16 and may not get it soon. If you forget the PIN and never set a recovery email, Google says the only way out is a factory reset. It cannot be turned on if the phone has a work profile or private space. Rules are changed on the phone itself; for remote changes Google points you to Family Link.

For a child's phone

If the phone already runs Android 17, try the built in controls first. They cost nothing and are part of the system. If the phone is older, Family Link needs a Google account for the child, and many families would rather not set that up. Ripple runs on Android 8.0 or later with no account. In v0.1.21 each app gets its own daily minutes, and 0 minutes keeps a game closed all day. You can block Shorts or Reels inside an app and leave the rest of it working. Loosening a rule or removing Ripple needs a 15 minute wait or the unlock code, and in strict mode only the code works and the app cannot be uninstalled on Android 11 or later. Keep the code yourself. Ripple has no parent app, no location and no reports, so you set rules while holding the phone.

For your own phone

Android 17's controls are built around a PIN that a parent knows. You can use them on your own phone if someone else sets the PIN, but they lock the whole phone at the limit. If you only want 20 minutes of Instagram a day, or a cap of 10 opens, Ripple works per app and per open. Give the strict mode code to a friend if you tend to undo your own limits.

iPhone

On iPhone, Screen Time in Settings already does most of this for free. Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5 and only blocks apps by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It has no daily minutes yet.
