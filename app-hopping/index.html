Hopping from one app to the next

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. A common complaint with app limits goes like this. You give each app 30 minutes. The video app runs out, so you open the next one, then a game, then a forum. Every limit holds, and you still spend three hours on the phone. Parents see the same thing when a child gets an hour for each of five apps.

Without any app

Write down the apps you actually drift into for one week. Most people find two or three. Move them off the home screen into a folder on the last page, and log out of the ones you can live without. A total for the whole day is easier to keep than a separate number for every app, because you only have to watch one figure.

Android, for yourself

Ripple for Android v0.1.27 (Android 8.0 or later) added a daily total shared by all the apps you limit. You turn it on in the App Limits page under "Shared by all limited apps" and pick a number, say 1 hour. Every limited app counts toward it. When the hour is used, all of those apps stay closed until 00:00. Each app's own daily limit still applies, and whichever runs out first blocks the app. The total is off by default.

Turning it on or making it shorter works as soon as you save. Making it longer or turning it off needs a wait of 15 minutes by default (30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours can be chosen) or an unlock code. The same version can show how long you used one app on each of the last 7 or 30 days, with the daily average and the busiest day, from usage data the app already stored. That helps you see which app the time actually goes to.

This feature is new. In v0.1.27 the block screen after the total runs out and the reset at midnight have been checked in tests but not yet on a real phone, so tell me if something looks wrong.

Android, for a child

Add the games and video apps your child uses and set one total, for example 1 hour on school days. Then the child chooses how to spend that hour, and switching apps does not add more time. A bedtime schedule and a homework schedule can sit on top of it. Keep the unlock code yourself and turn on strict mode, and Anti-Uninstall on Android 11 and later. Ripple has no remote management, so you set it up on the child's phone in person, and it is fair to tell the child what the total is.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is still v0.1.5 (iOS 16 or later). It blocks chosen apps during time windows, prevents deleting apps, resists clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no daily total, no per app daily limit and no usage page. The closest you can get is to put all the apps you hop between into the same schedule, for example evenings after 9 pm for yourself, or homework time for a child.
