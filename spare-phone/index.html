The old phone in the drawer

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. Many families have an old phone or two at home. Parents set up limits on the child's phone, and a few days later the child is watching videos on the old one over Wi-Fi. Adults do the same thing. The main phone locks at 11 pm, so the spare phone on the nightstand takes over.

Start with the phones themselves

Count every phone and tablet in the house that still turns on and can join Wi-Fi. A device you no longer use can be reset and given away, or kept in a place the child cannot reach, with the charger stored somewhere else. For your own spare phone, signing out of the video and social apps on it is often enough, because logging back in takes effort at midnight. If the router lets you, you can also stop the old device from joining Wi-Fi.

Android, for yourself

Any app blocker, Ripple included, only controls the phone it is installed on. Ripple has no account and no remote management, so each phone is set up on its own. If you really want the spare phone, install Ripple for Android v0.1.28 (Android 8.0 or later) on it too and copy the same rules by hand: a night schedule, daily limits for the apps you drift into, or one daily total shared by all limited apps. Making a rule looser needs a wait of 15 minutes by default (30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours can be chosen) or an unlock code.

Android, for a child

Set up the same rules on every Android device the child can reach, including the old phone and a tablet. Keep the unlock code yourself, turn on strict mode, and turn on Anti-Uninstall on Android 11 and later, so the app cannot simply be removed. Ripple also resists changing the clock to skip a schedule. Talk with the child about it. If the old phone shows up again, a calm talk about why the limit exists usually works better than a new trick.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is still v0.1.5 and needs iOS 16 or later, so a very old iPhone may not run it. It blocks chosen apps during time windows, prevents deleting apps, resists clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no daily limits and no daily total. An old iPhone that is stuck below iOS 16 is best reset and put away.
