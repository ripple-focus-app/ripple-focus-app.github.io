Minimalist launcher or app blocker?

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so read this with that in mind. Facts about Olauncher and minimalist phone come from their Google Play listings in November 2026.

What minimalist launchers do well

Olauncher replaces the Android home screen with a few app names in plain text. It is free, open source under GPLv3, has no ads and its listing says it collects no data. You can hide apps, rename them and open them with gestures. It does not block apps or set time limits.

minimalist phone has the same kind of text only home screen and adds an app blocker, blocking schedules, short video blocking inside YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and Snapchat, website blocking, a notification filter and grayscale. Its listing says it has monthly, yearly and one time plans, each with a free trial.

If icons and red badges are what pull you in, a plain home screen helps a lot, and Olauncher costs nothing.

Where a launcher stops

A hidden app can still be opened from search or from a notification, and going back to the stock launcher takes a few taps in Settings. On a child's phone that makes a launcher easy to undo, and it does not limit how long a game runs.

Your own Android phone

Ripple for Android (v0.1.20) is free with no ads and no account, and it works with any launcher, so you can keep Olauncher and add limits. Each app gets its own daily amount, and you can cap how many times a day it opens or block its website. Loosening a rule needs a 15 minute wait, or 30 minutes for apps like Douyin, or the unlock code. In strict mode only the code works, at most 3 successful uses a day, so a friend can set it and keep it. Ripple cannot hide apps and cannot block only Shorts or Reels.

A child's Android phone

Give games, cartoons and short video apps their own daily amounts, set one game to 0 minutes so it stays closed, and add homework and bedtime schedules. Turn on strict mode and keep the code yourself. Anti-Uninstall comes on with it (Android 11 or later), and changing the clock does not end a schedule. Ripple has no remote management, so rules are set on the child's phone.

iPhone

iPhone has no third party launchers. Ripple for iPhone (v0.1.5) only blocks apps by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It has no daily limits. For a child's iPhone, Screen Time with Family Sharing does more.
