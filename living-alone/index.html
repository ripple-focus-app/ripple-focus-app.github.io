Living alone, or parenting alone, and the phone

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker. When you live alone nobody sees how long you have been on the phone. You get home, eat with a video on, and it is past one before you put it down. A whole weekend can go the same way. Parents who look after a child mostly on their own have a different problem. The phone is the only way to get twenty quiet minutes to cook, and then the child does not want to give it back. Below is what I would set up. Ripple for Android is v0.1.21 and Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5, which has fewer features.

Calls and chats stay open

Living alone, you want people to be able to reach you. Ripple only blocks the apps you add. Calls, SMS and alarms are never blocked, and on Android WeChat can stay open with only Channels or Moments blocked.

If you live alone

Start with a night schedule such as 23:30 to 7:00 for short video, short drama and game apps. Then give the app that takes your evening a daily limit, for example 60 minutes. Once it is used up the app shows the block screen until the next day. An open limit helps with apps you keep opening for a quick look.

For weekends at home, add a schedule for part of the day, such as 10:00 to 13:00. If you decide to go out or read on the spot, quick focus locks the listed apps right away.

Without strict mode, loosening a rule needs a 15 minute wait (30 minutes for apps like Douyin) or the unlock code. With strict mode only the code works, at most 3 successful uses a day. Ask a friend or family member to set the code and keep it.

If you look after a child mostly by yourself

Handing over the phone so you can cook or shower happens to everyone. On Android give the cartoon, video and game apps a daily limit, such as 40 minutes each. When it runs out the block screen says no, so you do not have to.

Add a schedule for dinner and one for bedtime, such as 20:30 to 7:00. Calls keep working, so the child can still call you. Turn on strict mode and keep the code to yourself. Anti-Uninstall turns on with strict mode and needs Android 11 or later. Changing the clock does not end a schedule, and a forgotten code is lifted 72 hours after a request. Ripple has no remote management, so rules are set on the child's phone itself.

Once the child is asleep the evening is often the only time you have, and it tends to go on the phone. A night schedule on your own phone helps you get to bed.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, can stop apps being deleted, protects against clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no daily limits or open limits, so for a child's daily amounts use Screen Time with Family Sharing.
