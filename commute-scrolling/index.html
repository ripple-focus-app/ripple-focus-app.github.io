The commute is where the scrolling starts

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. A lot of people tell me the worst part of their day is the train or bus ride. You open a video app at the first stop and look up at your own. For kids the school bus or the ride home works the same way, and by the time they get in the door the evening has already started with an hour of videos.

Your own phone (Android)

If the ride happens at the same time every day, a schedule is the simplest setup. For example 8:00 to 9:00 and 18:00 to 19:00 on weekdays. During a schedule only the apps on your allowed list open, so put maps, the ticket or transit app, music and your chat app on the list and leave the video apps off. If you would rather keep some videos, give the video app a daily time limit instead, say 30 minutes, and once it is used up it stays closed until tomorrow.

On days with an odd timetable you can start a session by hand from the Today page. Loosening a rule takes a 15 minute wait by default, which is usually longer than the urge lasts. If you know you would wait it out, turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to someone else. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall (Android 11 or later).

A child's phone (Android)

Set the same kind of schedule for the trip to and from school, with the video apps and games off the list and calls, messages and maps on it. Keep the unlock code yourself. Changing the phone's clock does not end a schedule or reset a limit. Ripple has no remote control, so you set it up on the child's phone in person.

On iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is still v0.1.5 (iOS 16 or later). It blocks chosen apps during set hours, stops apps being deleted, protects against clock changes and uses an unlock code. Commute hours fit that well. It has no daily time limits or open limits, which are Android only for now.
