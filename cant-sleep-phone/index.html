When the apps are blocked at night and you still can't sleep

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. A bedtime schedule that closes video and game apps works well on the nights you are tired. On the nights you are not, you lie there, pick up the phone, see the block screen and start looking for a way around it. Kids do the same thing under the blanket.

What changed in Android v0.1.22

The schedule editor now has a switch called "Can't sleep? Listen for a while". It is on by default for schedules that run past midnight and off for daytime ones. When it is on, the block screen during that schedule shows a sleep card. Tapping it goes to the home screen first and then opens the sleep page, so the blocked app does not show when you go back.

The sleep page has rain, ocean, fire and white noise. You can set a timer, and the sound fades out over the last 2 minutes and then stops on its own. There is also slow breathing in a 4-7-8 or 4-4-6 rhythm, with sound and a light vibration if you want them. The rain, ocean and fire tracks are binaural recordings, so they sound wider with headphones. All the audio is CC0.

If the phone is yours

Put the apps you scroll in bed into a schedule, for example 23:00 to 7:00. Loosening the schedule takes a 15 minute wait by default (you can make the wait longer, up to 2 hours), or an unlock code. If you would sit out the wait, give the code to a partner or a friend and turn on strict mode so only the code works. When you open a blocked app out of habit, the sleep card is right there and you can start the rain instead.

If the phone is your child's

Set a school night schedule on the child's phone, for example 21:30 to 6:30, and keep the unlock code yourself. Videos and games stay closed. If your child can't sleep and opens one, the block screen offers rain sounds or slow breathing, and the sound stops when the timer ends. Changing the clock does not shorten the schedule, and Anti-Uninstall can be turned on (Android 11 or later). Ripple has no remote control, so you set it up on the child's phone in person.

What I haven't tested yet

Long playback with the screen off, incoming calls during playback and unplugging headphones have not been tested on a real phone in this build. If the sound behaves oddly on your phone, the email on the website reaches me.

On iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is still v0.1.5 (iOS 16 or later). It blocks chosen apps during set hours, stops apps being deleted, protects against clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no sleep sounds and no breathing page, so on iPhone you would pair the schedule with a separate sleep sound app that you leave off the block list.
