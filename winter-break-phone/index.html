Winter break, and the phone takes the whole day

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so keep that in mind. During term the school day puts a natural limit on the phone. Over winter break that limit is gone. Kids are home from morning to night, and a lot of adults have a week or two off with nothing planned, which usually means the couch and short videos.

If the phone is your child's (Android)

A daily time limit per app works better over a break than a fixed schedule, because the days have no shape. For example, give the video app 1 hour a day and a game 45 minutes. When the time is used up the app stays closed until the next day. If there is one game you do not want at all, set its limit to 0 minutes.

You can still add a night schedule, for example 22:00 to 7:00, so the break does not turn into staying up until 2am. Keep the unlock code yourself. Changing the phone's clock does not reset the limits, and Anti-Uninstall can be turned on (Android 11 or later) so the app cannot just be deleted. Ripple has no remote control, so you set it up on the child's phone in person, and changes later also happen on that phone.

If the phone is yours (Android)

The same daily limit helps on a long holiday. Pick the two or three apps that eat the afternoons and give them a total you would be happy with. Loosening a rule takes a 15 minute wait by default, which is often enough to change your mind. If you know you would sit out the wait, turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to someone in the family. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If the code gets lost, you can ask for release and it unlocks after 72 hours.

If the problem is opening an app dozens of times a day, the open limit lets you cap how many times an app can be opened per day.

On iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is still v0.1.5 (iOS 16 or later). It blocks chosen apps during set hours, stops apps being deleted, protects against clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no daily time limits, so over the break you would block by hours instead, for example video apps off until noon and again after 21:30. Apple's own Screen Time can add daily app limits on top.
