An app blocker you cannot just switch off

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker, so read this with that in mind. Facts about FocusMe come from focusme.com and Google Play, and facts about BlockerX from Google Play and the US App Store, in November 2026. Prices vary by country and change.

Most blockers work fine until the moment you want the app back. Then you open settings, turn it off, and the evening is gone. A child figures this out even faster. So the useful question is what each app does when someone tries to undo it.

FocusMe

FocusMe has apps for Android and iPhone that are free to download, and blocking, schedules and time limits work without an account. Each plan has a strictness level. The gentle ones make you type 5 or 10 random characters before stopping. Lock it Down wants 25 characters typed perfectly and then a wait. Enforced cannot be stopped until the plan ends. On Android, Tamper Protection has its own PIN and keeps FocusMe from being switched off or uninstalled. It can also limit how many times an app opens and how long each visit lasts, on iPhone too. Premium syncs plans with its Windows, Mac and Linux apps. A desktop licence is $17.95 a month, $119.40 a year or $449 once and includes 2 phones or tablets.

BlockerX

BlockerX is mainly an adult content filter, with safe search, YouTube restricted mode and word blocking, and it can also block apps, sites, social media and games. Its answer to switching it off is a person. An accountability partner is told if you uninstall it, and on iPhone they can get daily browsing reports. The Android app shows ads and has in app purchases. On iPhone a subscription is needed for all features, listed in the US at $49.99 a month, $139.99 a year or $149.99 once.

Ripple

Ripple is free, with no ads, no account and nothing sent off the phone. On Android v0.1.20, tightening a rule works at once. Loosening a rule or uninstalling needs a 15 minute wait or an unlock code, and rules on apps like Douyin and Kuaishou wait 30 minutes. In strict mode only the code works, at most 3 successful uses a day. Anti-Uninstall turns on with strict mode on Android 11 or later, and changing the phone clock does not end a schedule early. If the code is forgotten, a request lifts it after 72 hours. It also has schedules, a daily amount per app including 0 minutes, open limits, a break rule, quick focus, blocked websites and a usage calendar.

Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5 and does much less. It blocks apps by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It has no daily amounts.

For your own phone

If you want to give the code to a friend or partner and forget about it, Ripple's strict mode is built for that. If you also want the same rules on your laptop, FocusMe does that and Ripple does not. If your real problem is adult content, BlockerX is the one made for it. Ripple only blocks websites you type in and has no filter.

For a child's phone

On an Android phone, set daily amounts or 0 minutes for the games and video apps, turn on strict mode and keep the code. A homework schedule can start on its own on school days. Ripple has no parent app and no remote control, so you change rules on the child's phone. FocusMe's Tamper Protection PIN can also stay with the parent. For filtering adult sites, BlockerX or the free filter in iPhone Screen Time will do more than Ripple. On an iPhone or iPad, Screen Time with Family Sharing is free and has daily limits, and Ripple for iPhone only adds schedules with a code you keep.
