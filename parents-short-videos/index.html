Your parents scroll short videos until midnight

I am the developer of Ripple, a free app blocker for Android and iPhone with no ads and no account, so keep that in mind. A lot of adult children see the same thing on visits home. A parent who used to go to bed at nine now watches short videos until past midnight, eyes tired, volume up. Taking the phone away does not work, and the system screen time settings are hard for older people to find and easy to switch off. When the grandchildren spend the holidays with them, the kids end up scrolling next to the grandparents.

Talk first, then set it up together

This works better when your parent agrees. Sit with them, open the usage page and look at how long each app was used in the last 7 days. Pick one or two apps and one rule. A phone that suddenly refuses to open the app they like, with no explanation, tends to end with a call asking you to remove it.

What Ripple on Android can do (v0.1.31)

  • A daily time for each app, for example 60 minutes of the short video app. Since v0.1.25 each weekday can have its own time. Other apps, calls and messages stay normal.
  • A night window, for example 22:00 to 6:30, in which the chosen apps do not open.
  • If your parent watches videos inside WeChat, the WeChat Channels switch closes only that part. Chats, red packets and QR payments keep working.
  • In overnight windows the block page can show a sleep sounds card with rain, waves, fire and white noise on a timer, plus slow breathing. Playing with the screen off for a long time, incoming calls during playback and unplugging headphones have not been tested on a real phone yet.
  • Relaxing a rule waits 15 minutes by default (30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours are options), or takes the unlock code. Changing the phone clock does not add time.

Decide with your parent who keeps the unlock code. Some parents want to keep it themselves and only need the 15 minute wait as a pause. Ripple has no remote control, so changes have to be made on their phone, in person or with them following along on a video call.

Grandchildren staying over

If a child stays with grandparents for a holiday and brings their own Android phone, set it up before they go. Give games and short videos a daily time, add a bedtime window, and keep the unlock code yourself so the grandparents do not have to argue about it. On Android 11 and later turn on uninstall protection. The grandparents then only need to say "the phone decides".

Your own phone

The same setup works for you. Many adults who worry about their parents' screen time are scrolling the same apps at 1 a.m. Start with one app and a daily time a little below your 7 day average.

iPhone

Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5 and needs iOS 16 or later. It blocks chosen apps during time windows, stops deleting apps through Screen Time, resists clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no daily times, no WeChat Channels switch and no sleep sounds. For a parent with an iPhone, a night window that blocks the short video app is the simplest rule.

What it cannot do

Ripple only works on the phone it is installed on and does not filter websites. It is not in app stores yet, so it is installed from the website.
